# Business intent for the Multiticketing marketing website

This document defines the business intent and messaging constraints for the Multiticketing marketing website.
It is written for both humans and AI agents who will implement or modify the site.

If you are editing the site and your change conflicts with this document, update this document first.

## Truth sentence (must remain true)

Multiticketing is an all-in-one conference platform that replaces fragmented tools and improves attendee satisfaction end-to-end.

## What this website is for

- Primary job
  - Drive professional conference producers to watch a demo.
  - Then drive them to request access to the Dashboard.
  - Once invited, they create an event shell (name, dates, venue) as the first success moment.
- The conversion story
  - Demo -> Request access -> Dashboard (invite-only) -> Create event shell.

## What this website is not for

- Do not position the product as "open source" or "free".
- Do not position the product as "ticketing-only".
- Do not optimize the homepage for contributors or GitHub stars.
- Do not lead with the foundation story as the primary pitch.

## Audience and buyer

- Primary audience
  - Professional event organizers and conference producers.
- Secondary audience
  - Operations and registration leads who feel the pain of fragmented tools.
- Who we are not optimizing for on the homepage
  - Developers looking primarily for an open-source project.

## Category and positioning

- Category label to use prominently
  - Conference operations platform.
- Brand name
  - Multiticketing.
- How to describe the suite
  - All-in-one conference platform.
  - Replaces the Frankenstein stack of ticketing, forms, check-in tools, spreadsheets, and chat.

## Core narrative

- The enemy
  - Chaos caused by fragmented tools that creates unhappy attendees.
- The wedge
  - A complete attendee experience loop that can be operated and measured end-to-end.
- The loop (must show up in messaging)
  - Register -> Personal agenda -> Interact -> Feedback -> Analytics.

## Analytics promise (this is "now", not "eventually")

Analytics is part of the product's core value.
This site should communicate that Multiticketing provides all of the following.

- Live ops
  - Check-in pace and operational bottlenecks.
  - Track and room utilization.
- Attendee experience quality
  - Session feedback and satisfaction signals.
  - Engagement and participation indicators.
- Program planning
  - What to keep, cut, and schedule differently next time.
- Stakeholder and sponsor reporting
  - Outcome reporting that is credible without custom spreadsheet work.

## Integrations stance

Multiticketing replaces the Frankenstein stack, and it can integrate alongside ticketing-only tools.
The site should not force a "rip and replace everything" posture.

- Ticketing-only examples to reference
  - Luma.
  - Eventbrite.

## Commercial model

- Professionals
  - Enterprise, negotiated contracts (sales-led).
  - Tiered pricing based on per-event and attendee headcount.
- GDG chapters
  - Offered free worldwide.
  - This should be presented as a secondary band after the professional pitch.
  - Do not let the GDG offer redefine Multiticketing as a free community-only tool.

## Trust strategy (since we are not leading with free/open-source)

- Primary trust
  - Social proof.
  - "See it yourself" via a demo.
- Secondary trust
  - Integration and reliability reassurances.
  - Objection handling content later on the page.

## CTA hierarchy

The site should support these calls-to-action in this order.

1. Watch demo (primary).
2. Go to Dashboard (request access) (secondary).
3. Contact sales (tertiary).

## Qualification stance for "request access"

Qualification should be light on the website.
Use seriousness signals rather than hard numerical gates.

- Minimum bar
  - Real organization identity.
  - Real event timeframe.
- Hard qualification
  - Happens later in the sales process.

## "Why now" triggers (messaging should resonate with all)

- Scaling complexity (primary emphasis).
- "Never again" after a burned event.
- Reporting pressure from stakeholders and sponsors.
- Recurring series needing a repeatable operating system.

## Implementation guardrails for agents

- Replace any occurrences of "open source" and "free" as the primary identity.
  - The only "free" claim allowed is the GDG chapters exception, and it must not appear as the hero tagline.
- The hero section must reflect the attendee journey loop and the suite positioning.
- The demo CTA must be the primary action on the homepage.
- The Dashboard CTA must be framed as request access or invite-only, not self-serve signup.
- If a page or section discusses integrations, it should support the "alongside ticketing-only tools" stance.
